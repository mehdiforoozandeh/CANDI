# PREDICTD

PREDICTD is the paper that made the 3-D tensor the field's default mental model, and the first to impute every missing entry from a single joint factorisation rather than one model per target.

## Method

Roadmap and ENCODE data are arranged as a 3-D tensor with axes **cell type × assay × genomic position** (`raw/durham-2018-predictd.xml`). The tensor is "long and skinny": far fewer cell types and assays than positions. PREDICTD factorises it into per-axis latent factor matrices, so that a missing entry is reconstructed from the product of its cell-type, assay, and position factors. Training is distributed over a cloud/Spark cluster — the "parallel" in the name — which is what makes a genome-scale factorisation tractable.

## Results

- Lower overall **MSE** than [[chromimpute]] (`raw/ernst-2015-chromimpute.xml`), and combining the two methods improves further, indicating complementary error profiles (`raw/durham-2018-predictd.xml`). "Overall" is load-bearing: the later three-way comparison in [[avocado]] shows ChromImpute still best on MSE1obs, the top 1% of positions by observed signal.
- Explicitly evaluated in the regime that matters: cell types with **only one or two available assays**, where the paper reports the model still performs well. This is the same concern later formalised in [[cross-cell-type-generalization-pitfall]].
- The learned factors are interpretable: cell-type factors group related biosamples, assay factors group related marks, position factors track genomic annotation.
- Imputed data recovers enhancer activity at noncoding human accelerated regions.

## What it establishes for later work

The three-axis factor structure is inherited directly by [[avocado]], which replaces the multilinear product with a neural network and adds multi-scale genomic factors. The weakness PREDICTD shares with all joint-factorisation methods is that denoising a *specific* observed experiment requires holding it out and refitting — the property that distinguishes [[chromimpute]].

Relative to the discriminative per-target approach, tensor factorisation makes an explicit **low-rank** assumption: that the whole compendium is generated by a modest number of latent cell-type and assay programmes. Whether that assumption holds for poorly characterised cell types is precisely what [[encode-imputation-challenge]] found hard to measure.
