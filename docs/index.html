CANDI: Self-Supervised and Confidence-Aware Denoising Imputation of Genomic Data

Mehdi Foroozandeh · Abdul Rahman Diab · Maxwell Libbrecht — School of Computing Science, Simon Fraser University

Top: the data tensor is cell types × assays × genome. One experiment is one thread along the genome axis, seen end-on as a square on the front face and edge-on as a lane on the top and right faces; grey threads are missing experiments. The tensor shows availability, not signal. Slicing out one cell type gives an assays × genome matrix with holes, and CANDI reads one 30 kb window of it as raw read counts, plus the DNA sequence and the experiment's covariates. The bottom row runs back the other way: the same window with a mean and a 95% interval at every position, then the same slice and tensor with the holes filled. Schematic. A Observed coverage against stated confidence for DNase-seq; the model is overconfident at low confidence levels and converges on nominal coverage as confidence rises. B Gene-expression prediction (Pearson r) against the number of assays available in a cell type. C Reproducibility of chromatin state (SAGA) annotations, measured as the SAGAconf robustness ratio — the fraction of the genome receiving a confident, reproducible state — across six held-out biosamples. Panels replot published results from the MLCB manuscript.
